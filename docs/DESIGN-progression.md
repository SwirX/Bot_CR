# RoboticsCMC progression — full design spec

Everything below is **design only**. Nothing here is implemented. The numbers
are generated by `scripts/preview_progression.py`, which is the single place
they live, and the shape of its `build()` output is the payload proposed for the
`mc_progression` Appwrite table so that agreeing with this document is agreeing
with the stored document.

---

## 1. Level curve

Per-level cost is flat inside a 10-level band and grows `1.42x` at each
boundary. The first band is split out as a cheap tutorial ramp.

| level | cost into level | cumulative XP | R | milestone R |
|------:|-----------------:|--------------:|--:|------------:|
| 10 | 150 | 1,350 | 100 | 500 |
| 20 | 800 | 9,350 | 150 | 1,000 |
| 30 | 1,136 | 20,710 | 200 | 1,500 |
| 40 | 1,613 | 36,840 | 250 | 2,000 |
| 50 | 2,291 | 59,750 | 300 | 2,500 |
| 60 | 3,253 | 92,280 | 350 | 3,000 |
| 70 | 4,619 | 138,470 | 400 | 3,500 |
| 80 | 6,559 | 204,060 | 450 | 4,000 |
| 90 | 9,313 | 297,190 | 500 | 4,500 |
| 100 | 13,225 | 429,440 | 550 | 5,000 |

**Total: 429,440 XP.** A level at 70 costs 49% of the entire 0-20 run, down
from 89% before the tutorial band was split out.

### Earning rate and time

XP income is **not flat**. Cost grows `1.42x` per band, so income grows `1.1x`
per band — time-per-level rises gently instead of exploding.

| level | XP for band | XP/hour | time this level | cumulative |
|------:|------------:|--------:|----------------:|-----------:|
| 10 | 1,350 | 1,500 | 0.1h | 0.9h |
| 20 | 8,000 | 1,650 | 0.5h | 5.7h |
| 30 | 11,360 | 1,815 | 0.6h | 12.0h |
| 40 | 16,130 | 1,997 | 0.8h | 20.1h |
| 50 | 22,910 | 2,196 | 1.0h | 30.5h |
| 60 | 32,530 | 2,416 | 1.3h | 44.0h |
| 70 | 46,190 | 2,657 | 1.7h | 61.4h |
| 80 | 65,590 | 2,923 | 2.2h | 83.8h |
| 90 | 93,130 | 3,215 | 2.9h | 112.8h |
| 100 | 132,250 | 3,537 | 3.7h | **150.2h** |

150 active hours to level 100 — about five months at one hour a day. This is
the alternative to flat income, which reaches 100 in the same 150 hours but
makes the last level cost 8.8 hours of pure mining and turns level 90 into a
wall nobody crosses.

### Prestige

Past level 100 the rewards track ends but levels continue. A reward every 50
levels, quadratic, because Minecraft has no season to compete against and
nothing to reset for.

| level | 150 | 200 | 250 | 300 | 350 |
|---|-----|-----|-----|-----|-----|
| R | 5,000 | 20,000 | 45,000 | 80,000 | 125,000 |

## 2. Reward track

Three tiers. One is not enough to hold attention over years.

| cadence | reward | purpose |
|---|---|---|
| every level | R, scaled (`50 + 5x level`) | the bar always moves |
| every 5th | cosmetic | visible to other players, free to the economy |
| every 10th | milestone unlock + job perk | permanent convenience that compounds |

**Total R across 1-100: 57,750.**

### Milestone unlocks

Permanent, not percentage nudges — a +5% stops mattering once everyone has it.

| level | unlock |
|---|---|
| 10 | home slot 1 · +0.5% shop discount |
| 20 | home slot 2 · +0.5% shop discount |
| 30 | home slot 3 · claim radius +1 · +0.5% discount |
| 40 | home slot 4 · +0.5% shop discount |
| 50 | home slot 5 · claim radius +1 · AFK daily cap +1h · claim cost −25% |
| 60 | home slot 6 · +0.5% shop discount |
| 70 | home slot 7 · claim radius +1 · +0.5% discount |
| 80 | home slot 8 · +0.5% shop discount |
| 90 | home slot 9 · +0.5% shop discount |
| 100 | home slot 10 · title "Peak" · permanent +1% shop discount |

### Cosmetics (every 5)

Chat tags, particle trails, join-message colours at 5/15/25/35/45/55/65/75/85/95.
Deliberately **not monetised** — they are the visible half of progression and if
they cost money the track becomes a bill.

### Job perks (10/25/50/75)

| job | 10 | 25 | 50 | 75 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| lumberjack | +5% log yield | +10% | +15% | +20% |
| miner | +5% ore drop | +10% | +15% | +20% |
| builder | place freely in your own claim | +5% sell | +10% sell | +15% sell |
| hunter | +5% mob drops | +10% | +15% | +20% |
| farmer | auto-replant | +10% crops | +20% | +30% |
| blacksmith | 10% faster smelting | 20% | 30% | 40% |
| crafter | +25% craft XP | +50% | +75% | +100% |
| businessman | +10% daily sell cap | +20% | +30% | +40% |

Crafter buys XP back rather than adding output, because its `0.1` XP weight
makes it the grindliest job by rate.

## 3. Job XP rebalance

Today both Hunter and Crafter have the problem the player described: a single
rate for everything, forever.

**Hunter today:** `8.0 x rarity`, where rarity is `20` for dragon/warden, `15`
for wither, `5` for blaze/ghast/enderman and **`1` for everything else**. A cow
and a skeleton pay identically, at every level, forever.

**Crafter today:** `2.0 x 2.0 multiplier x 0.1 weight = 0.4 XP` for *every*
recipe. Crafting planks pays the same as crafting an Elytra, so the optimal
strategy is spam the cheapest recipe in the game.

Both become tiered, and every tier has a **level at which it stops paying**.
This is the core anti-farming change: progress up the ladder or stop earning.

### Hunter tiers

| tier | examples | XP | stops at |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 0 fodder | cow, pig, sheep, chicken, rabbit, squid, bat | 2 | L15 |
| 1 common hostile | zombie, spider, creeper, skeleton, slime | 8 | L25 |
| 2 uncommon | husk, drowned, witch, pillager, cave spider, ravager | 20 | L40 |
| 3 rare | blaze, ghast, enderman, phantom, guardian, evoker | 60 | L60 |
| 4 elite | wither, ender dragon, warden | 400 | never |
| 5 quest | boss/rare targets | quest only | quest only |

Killing cows stops paying at level 15. Skeleton farming stops at 25. The only
sustainable path above 60 is hunting things that are actually hard.

### Crafter tiers

| tier | examples | XP | stops at |
|---|---|---:|---|
| 0 trivial | planks, sticks, torches, ladders, slabs, stairs, panes, buttons | **0** | never |
| 1 basic | stone tools/armour, wooden furniture, chests, crafting table | 4 | L25 |
| 2 intermediate | iron tools/armour, stone bricks, blast furnace, hoppers | 12 | L40 |
| 3 advanced | diamond tools/armour, enchanting table, anvil, shulker box | 35 | L60 |
| 4 master | enchanted books, elytra, modded rare recipes | 90 | never |
| 5 quest | named challenge crafts | quest only | quest only |

Tier 0 is the important one: **planks and sticks pay nothing, ever, at any
level.** Right now they pay 0.4 XP each and a player can sit in a 2x2 crafting
grid generating them forever.

### Quest crafts

Rare recipes are removed from passive XP entirely and become explicit goals:
craft an Elytra, a full diamond set, a beacon, a shulker box, a dragon-breather.
Each is one-time, hand-authored, and pays R + XP + sometimes a unique unlock.
They exist because the highest-value crafts are also the rarest, and passive XP
alone would either make them worth nothing or farmable.

## 4. Slash-command unlockables

Two stages, deliberately:

1. A **level milestone** grants eligibility (the command appears for you)
2. The player **buys the unlock** with R, permanently
3. Every **use** then costs R

Stage 1 makes it a goal. Stage 2 makes it a decision. Stage 3 makes it a
sink rather than a one-off purchase.

| command | unlocked at | unlock cost | per-use cost | scaled by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `/chest` | L20 | 5,000 R | 50 R per slot | **Builder** |
| `/furnace` | L30 | 8,000 R | 20 R per smelt | **Farmer** |
| `/craft` | L40 | 12,000 R | 15 R x recipe tier | **Crafter** |
| `/shop` extra slots | L25 | 10,000 R | — | **Businessman** |

### Why Builder owns `/chest`

Storage is an extension of territory — it is where you put what you mined and
where you claim you are. `/chest` cost per slot falls with Builder level
(`50 x 0.97^level`, floor 10 R), so levelling Builder makes logistics cheap.
This gives Builder a reason to exist beyond placing blocks, since it currently
has only an XP axis.

### Why Farmer owns `/furnace`

Cooking is the step after harvesting. Farmer already owns the harvest XP
(`harvest-xp: 4.0`); the furnace is the next link in that chain. Cost per smelt
falls with Farmer level (`20 x 0.97^level`, floor 5 R).

### Businessman

Businessman has **no XP source and no function at all** today — it exists as a
`SkillType` with an XP column that nothing writes. Its first real perk is
concurrent sell slots: each Businessman level grants an extra slot in the
`/shop` sell window, so a player can list several item types at once instead of
one at a time. That is a genuine convenience that only pays if you trade often,
which is what a job called Businessman should reward.

## 5. Solo income

**Question: is rare-ore-to-money working?**

Yes. `MarketService.sell` resolves the held stack's `Material` to a shop listing
and pays `sellPrice x quantity`. Ores are priced:

| item | buy | sell (0.35 ratio) |
|---|---:|---:|
| iron ingot | 140 | 49 R |
| gold ingot | 90 | 31 R |
| diamond | 900 | 315 R |
| coal | (priced) | — |

Capped at **40,000 R/day**. So a player alone can mine diamond and bank 315 R
each with no other player involved. That works.

**What is missing:** a floor. `/sell` only pays for items in the shop, and the
shop is 12 items. Everything else — most mobs, most crops, most crafted goods —
has no value at all. A player whose build does not produce those 12 items has
no solo path.

Fix is the market maker: the server always buys at `sell_price x (1 - spread)`
regardless of other players, funded by an activity-scaled mint so the treasury
does not go bankrupt on a quiet week.

## 6. Mid-game goals

Between level 20 and level 70 a player needs reasons to log in that are not
"the number went up".

1. **Daily quest board** — 3 rotating quests, resets daily. Always a reason to
   appear today regardless of level. Pools from the tier systems above.
2. **Weekly challenges** — 1 harder quest, resets weekly, pays materially more.
3. **Server-wide goals** — "the server mines 10,000 ore" unlocks something for
   *everyone*. Makes a quiet server feel alive because solo work counts.
4. **Job mastery** — reaching level 50 in any single job unlocks a job-specific
   endgame perk, so specialising has a destination.
5. **Collection achievements** — "sell 500 diamond", "craft one of each tier 4".
6. **Command unlocks** — the R sinks above give R somewhere to go.

## 7. Retention — honest reflection

**Today it is bad, and not for the reason it looks like.** The curve is not the
problem. The problem is that there is no reason to come back tomorrow:

- levelling changes one number on one screen
- nothing scales with level except the cost of levelling
- no daily content
- no reason for another player to exist

In rough order of impact:

1. **Income scales with level (modelled above).** Levelling makes you earn
   faster, so there is a reason to return. This is the single biggest change.
2. **Daily quests.** A reason to log in today that has nothing to do with level.
3. **Server goals.** A reason that is not about you.
4. **Economy floor.** Always a way to earn, so an offline player is never stuck.
5. **Command unlocks.** New capability as a visible goal.

**After the modded client lands**, retention improves further for one reason
that no server-side change can match: a real GUI showing the exact XP bar, what
the next milestone unlocks, and active quests turns a grind into visible
progress. The numbers matter less when the presentation is good.

## 8. Timing the modded client

The client should come **after** this spec is locked and server-side, not
before. Three reasons:

1. **The client renders the progression.** If the curve and rewards are not
   final, the UI is written twice. Building the spec first means the client is
   written once against a stable contract.
2. **Mods will add XP sources.** A mod that grants XP has to plug into the tier
   system, not invent its own scale — otherwise one mod with a generous reward
   invalidates the whole curve. That is only enforceable once the tier system
   exists.
3. **It is a continuation, not a reset.** Same map, same database. The
   progression, economy, claims, homes and jobs all carry over, which is the
   whole point of doing this properly now.

Recommended order:

1. Lock this spec
2. `mc_progression` table + server-side curve/rewards reading from it
3. Job XP rebalance (hunter and crafter tiers) — this is what makes new XP
   sources from mods pluggable later
4. Quest framework + daily board
5. Command unlockables and economy floor
6. **Then** the modded client, rendering against the same `mc_progression` rows

Step 3 before the client matters more than it looks: the tier system is the
interface mods will code against.
